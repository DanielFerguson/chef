# M4.1 — Natural Plan-to-Shop reliability

Status: implemented, audited, and complete on 16 July 2026.

M4.1 corrects the boundary between planning and shopping. The existing M4
shopping domain can aggregate, edit, revise, budget, match, and complete a
structured list, but the first real plan exposed a product-level gap: Chef
selected seven ordinary cookable meals as title-only custom meals. None had an
attached recipe version, so Shopping asked the household to manually provide
seven ingredient lists.

That is not the intended Chef experience. A household may review or correct a
draft, but it should not have to reconstruct the recipes behind meals Chef
recommended.

## Decision summary

- Conversation remains the primary interface across planning and shopping.
- Selecting a cookable meal causes Chef to prepare a structured draft recipe.
- Every cookable planned meal must have an exact `RecipeVersion` before the plan
  is ready to leave review.
- Chef automatically derives and aggregates shopping requirements from those
  recipe versions.
- Eating out, takeaway, intentionally open meals, and linked leftovers are
  explicit non-recipe states and do not require an invented recipe.
- The budget is optional context, not the first blocking shopping task.
- Manual ingredient entry remains an advanced recovery path for generation
  failures or deliberately unusual meals. It is never the normal happy path.
- Existing confirmed plans receive the same recipe-preparation workflow rather
  than being stranded in the old manual-resolution interface.

M4.1 is a prerequisite for M5. Cooking mode cannot be trustworthy if selected
meals do not already carry ingredients, steps, equipment, and preparation
notices.

## Product invariant

Every selected meal must resolve to exactly one of these states:

1. **Cookable recipe** — the `PlannedMeal` retains an immutable
   `RecipeVersion` with usable ingredients and steps.
2. **Linked leftovers** — the meal identifies its source planned meal and does
   not create a second full ingredient requirement.
3. **Explicit non-recipe meal** — takeaway, eating out, or intentionally open,
   chosen visibly by the household.

A meal title and summary alone are not a usable recipe. `custom` is not an
implicit escape hatch from recipe preparation for an ordinary cookable meal.

Chef may estimate culinary ingredients and quantities. Those estimates must be
editable and clearly presented as a Chef-prepared draft until used. This does
not weaken the safety boundary: Chef must apply explicit household constraints
and must never invent, infer, or silently relax an allergy or medical rule.

## Happy-path experience

The household continues in the same meal-plan conversation.

### While exploring

Chef can propose lightweight meal ideas without generating a complete recipe
for every option:

> Here are four quick dinners that fit Tuesday. Which one sounds best?

Proposal cards contain enough information to choose: title, short explanation,
estimated time, estimated cost when available, and relevant constraint fit.

### When a meal is selected

Once the household chooses an ordinary cookable meal, Chef records the
selection and prepares its recipe in the background:

> I’ve added chicken katsu curry to Saturday and I’m preparing its recipe.
> Sunday and Wednesday are still open.

The calendar card can show a quiet `Preparing recipe` state. The household can
continue planning while preparation runs.

### Before confirmation

After the final selection, Chef derives readiness from structured state:

> Your seven dinners are selected. I’m finishing one recipe, then I’ll show you
> the complete plan to confirm.

Confirmation becomes available only when every slot has participants and every
cookable meal has a usable recipe version. The household is not asked to
approve every ingredient line before confirming; recipes are editable drafts,
and targeted clarification is reserved for material ambiguity.

### Plan-to-Shop handoff

After explicit confirmation:

> Your plan is confirmed. I’m combining the recipe ingredients into one
> shopping list now.

The structured workspace shows calm progress such as:

```text
Preparing shopping list
7 recipes ready
Combining ingredients
```

When complete, the same conversation continues:

> Your shopping list is ready: 36 recipe ingredients have been combined into
> 24 items. Would you like to review what you already have, add household
> extras, or set a budget?

The user can reply naturally:

- `We already have rice, soy sauce, and oil.`
- `Add three litres of milk and two loaves of white toast bread.`
- `Make Saturday serve four instead.`
- `Try to keep the shop below $180.`
- `Use the cheapest sensible Coles products.`

Each turn invokes authorised domain actions and visibly updates the structured
list. The conversation never becomes the sole store of shopping state.

## Progressive Shopping workspace

Shopping exposes only the next useful action.

### 1. Preparing

Show:

- the plan and date span;
- recipe and list-generation progress;
- pause, retry, or return-to-plan actions when applicable;
- the persistent plan conversation composer.

Hide:

- the empty item editor;
- budget forms;
- product matching;
- `Complete shop`;
- seven expanded manual ingredient forms.

### 2. Review ingredients

Show the generated, aggregated list and ask the household to:

- mark items already in the pantry;
- correct quantities or units;
- exclude optional items;
- add household staples and non-recipe requests;
- inspect the source meals when useful.

The primary action is `Review shopping list`. Budget is an optional secondary
section or a conversational suggestion after the list has meaningful content.

### 3. Budget and product preparation

After list review, Chef can:

- apply a household budget default or plan override;
- show a clearly labelled estimated total and unpriced-item count;
- use remembered brand, pack-size, and substitution preferences;
- prepare product choices for later retailer order placement.
- After an approved product plan and cart, fulfilment selection and order
  confirmation happen in Chef (M6.2); card details are never collected.

### 4. Ready and completed

`Complete shop` appears only when the list has included items, no unresolved
recipe preparation, and no stale plan changes. Cart preparation remains M6;
fulfilment selection and order confirmation remain explicit Chef UI actions
before any retailer submit.

## Domain design

### Recipe-required classification

Recipe preparation is required for a planned meal when it is:

- a selected family recipe, which already has a version;
- an accepted Chef proposal for an ordinary cookable meal;
- a user-named custom meal that Chef can reasonably draft.

Recipe preparation is not required for:

- `takeaway`;
- `eating_out`;
- `open`;
- `leftovers` with a valid source planned meal.

If a custom name is materially ambiguous, Chef asks one focused conversational
question. A preparation failure creates a visible retry state; it does not
silently downgrade the meal to manual data entry.

### Chef-owned drafting boundary

Introduce a Chef-owned `MealPlanRecipeDrafter` interface so the Laravel AI SDK
remains replaceable. One typed request is created only after every meal slot is
filled, and contains every selected cookable meal plus:

- meal title and summary;
- planned servings and timing expectations;
- household stated preferences;
- explicit safety constraints;
- available plan context needed to avoid accidental duplication;
- a bounded snapshot of the durable plan conversation for plan-level timing,
  nutrition, ingredient, and variety intent;
- provenance identifiers for the selected proposal or user turn.

Its typed output contains an exact-once recipe result for every selected meal,
including:

- title and summary;
- base servings;
- preparation and cooking minutes;
- ingredients with quantities, units, preparation, and optional state;
- ordered steps and optional timers;
- equipment;
- preparation notices.

The production adapter uses Laravel AI SDK structured output with GPT-5.6 Sol
and high reasoning. Normal tests use a deterministic fake and never call
OpenAI. Conversation context is stored in the durable batch input but excluded
from the structural fingerprint, so later chat does not invalidate in-flight
work.

### Durable preparation state

Store one durable recipe-generation state on the team-owned `MealPlan` with:

- `meal_plan_id`, `team_id`, and requesting user;
- `status`: pending, processing, completed, or failed;
- an input fingerprint and attempt count;
- safe failure code and message fields;
- timestamps for start and completion.

The record makes progress, retries, support diagnostics, and browser refreshes
durable. It must not retain raw model reasoning. Provider request identifiers
may be retained only according to the application’s later privacy and
retention policy.

### Reusable actions and jobs

Add narrow domain/application capabilities:

- `PrepareMealPlanRecipes` waits until every slot is resolved, fingerprints the
  whole draft, and dispatches one idempotent job.
- `MaterializeMealPlanRecipes` validates exact coverage of every missing
  cookable meal, invokes the existing recipe creation/version actions, and
  attaches each immutable version atomically.
- `PrepareMealPlanShoppingList` waits for recipe readiness and then invokes the
  existing `GenerateShoppingList` action.

Selection invokes the shared plan-level preparation action. The action remains
idle while any slot is open or awaiting a proposal, then the final selection
dispatches the single long-running drafting job. HTTP controls, AI tools, jobs,
console commands, and future MCP tools therefore follow the same path.

Recipe creation uses an idempotency key derived from the team, planned meal,
and preparation input fingerprint. A retry must not create a second recipe or
plan revision.

### Readiness and failure behavior

Extend plan readiness with:

- `recipes_required`;
- `recipes_ready`;
- `recipes_preparing`;
- `recipes_failed`;
- a next action such as `prepare_recipes`, `retry_recipe`, or
  `review_and_confirm`.

`GenerateShoppingList` must not silently ignore an ordinary cookable meal with
no recipe. It should return or throw a typed preparation-required result unless
called through the orchestrator that prepares the missing recipes first.

Existing explicit manual `ShoppingListMealResolution` data remains valid and
traceable. The manual editor moves behind a recovery action such as `Add
ingredients manually` for a failed or deliberately exceptional meal.

## Conversation integration

The Shopping route remains a structured workspace but belongs to the same
`MealPlan` and `Conversation`. It must expose the same composer and persist new
messages in the existing thread.

Add narrow AI tools around existing or new actions:

- inspect shopping preparation and list state;
- prepare or regenerate the shopping list;
- add one or more manual or staple items as one atomic batch;
- update quantity, unit, note, pantry, and include/exclude state;
- set or remove the plan budget;
- inspect product preferences and matches.

Tool results return concise state summaries and the next useful action. The
assistant must acknowledge tool-only turns visibly, just as it does during
planning. Direct list manipulation remains available and authoritative.

Shopping additions are merge-safe and idempotent at the user-message and item
boundary. The whole batch validates before any row is written, then commits as
one list revision. A replay returns the existing rows, while a later retry adds
only normalised item names still missing from the list. OpenAI parallel tool
calls are disabled for the mixed read/write Chef agent, and a provider failure
after a committed mutation is recovered from durable revisions so the user is
never told that a successful write failed.

## Existing-plan recovery

Confirmed plans created before M4.1 may contain cookable custom meals without
recipe versions. Visiting Shopping or asking Chef to prepare the list should:

1. identify those meals;
2. create durable preparation records;
3. prepare and attach versioned recipes;
4. regenerate the list from those versions;
5. retain manual and staple rows already added by the household;
6. retain traceability and record a new list revision;
7. explain the recovery in the conversation.

Do not call OpenAI from a database migration. Backfill is an authorised,
observable, retryable application workflow.

For meal plan 1, the expected result is seven attached recipe versions and one
populated aggregated list without requiring the household to type seven
ingredient lists.

## Data integrity and safety

- Scope every preparation, recipe, planned meal, and list query to the active
  authorised team.
- Add policy and cross-team isolation coverage for the new preparation model.
- Validate structured output before creating a recipe version.
- Reject empty recipes and invalid servings, units, positions, or quantities.
- Preserve the exact recipe version used by a confirmed plan.
- Mark derived shopping data stale if a recipe-bearing confirmed plan changes.
- Apply explicit safety constraints to drafting context without converting
  preferences or inferred feedback into allergies.
- Treat recipe drafts as untrusted model output until schema and domain
  validation succeed.
- Do not expose raw provider errors or sensitive prompt context in the household
  UI.

## Exit evidence required

M4.1 is complete only when:

- a naturally planned ordinary meal always has a usable attached recipe before
  Plan review can finish;
- a confirmed natural-language plan produces a populated, traceable shopping
  list without manual ingredient reconstruction;
- the household can make pantry, quantity, staple, and budget changes through
  the same conversation and see the structured list update;
- Shopping shows one clear next action at each state;
- existing confirmed plans can be recovered through the application workflow;
- failures are retryable, visible, and do not create duplicate recipes or list
  rows;
- all tenancy, safety, responsive, and full-repository gates pass with recorded
  evidence.

M5 does not begin until this evidence is satisfied and M4 is marked complete
again.

## Implementation evidence

The implementation follows the design in this document:

- `MealPlan` provides durable, team-scoped pending, processing, completed, and
  failed recipe-generation state for the complete draft.
- `MealPlanRecipeDrafter` isolates one GPT-5.6 Sol high-reasoning structured
  output request from the Chef domain; deterministic fakes keep ordinary tests
  offline.
- one shared queued job prepares, validates, materialises, retries, and recovers
  all required exact recipe versions idempotently;
- plan readiness and shopping generation cannot silently pass an unresolved
  cookable meal, while explicit manual resolution remains a traceable advanced
  recovery path;
- Shopping retains the original plan conversation and exposes natural-language
  tools for preparation, inspection, staples, pantry and quantity changes, and
  budgets;
- the progressive React/Inertia workspace renders preparation, failure,
  review, optional budget, stale, completion, and narrow-screen states;
- a deterministic browser journey proves typed planning through conversational
  shopping, and an authorised legacy-plan regression proves queued recovery.

Final evidence is recorded in [MILESTONES.md](MILESTONES.md): 145 backend tests
with 826 assertions at 86.1% application coverage, 21 browser journeys with 166
assertions, clean backend/frontend/build/audit/migration gates, React Doctor at
100/100, and a live warning-free desktop and narrow-screen inspection.

## Implemented decisions

M4.1 implements the following approved defaults:

1. Generate a recipe only after a cookable meal is selected, not for every
   suggestion.
2. Let households edit Chef-prepared drafts without requiring line-by-line
   approval before confirmation.
3. Keep Shopping in the same durable conversation, with the structured list as
   the primary visual artifact.
4. Keep manual ingredient entry only as an explicit recovery option.
5. Treat the budget as optional and reveal it after the first useful list
   exists.
